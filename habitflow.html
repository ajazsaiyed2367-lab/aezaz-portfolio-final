01 · Overview

Habit tracking, stripped down to what matters.

Most habit-tracking apps ask for an account before you've even tracked your first habit — then layer in subscriptions, streak-shaming push notifications, and cloud dashboards nobody asked for. HabitFlow was designed to do the opposite: open the app, check in, done.

The entire product — from UX research through final PWA build — was created using an AI-driven development workflow, with every prompt and decision documented alongside the code.

"The goal wasn't to build more features — it was to prove a habit tracker only needs a handful of taps to earn its place on someone's home screen."

02 · Research & Discovery

Understanding why habit trackers get abandoned

Research centered on a primary persona — students and young professionals juggling 2–5 personal routines — and the recurring reasons they drop off existing habit apps: forced sign-ups, paywalls, and overloaded dashboards.

01

Speed over features

Daily check-ins needed to complete in under 3 seconds with a maximum of two taps.

02

No forced accounts

Sign-ups and cloud logins were consistently cited as reasons people abandoned similar apps.

03

Data ownership

Storing data on-device rather than in the cloud builds trust and removes a point of friction.

04

Visual motivation

Simple streaks and a monthly heatmap motivate consistency better than dense dashboards.

Research protocol: 5–8 participant interviews on prior habit-app abandonment, followed by 5-participant usability testing on core tasks (create, complete, view streak, export data).

03 · Problem

How might we make daily check-ins effortless without asking anything of the user first?

The challenge was designing a product that earns trust and habit-forming behaviour immediately — with zero onboarding friction, no login wall, and complete reliability even with no network connection.

01

Onboarding friction

Every extra step between opening the app and logging a habit is a reason to abandon it.

02

Offline reliability

A habit tracker that fails without signal defeats its own purpose — check-ins happen anywhere.

03

Trust through privacy

No account, no analytics, no cloud sync — data ownership needed to be a visible product decision, not a footnote.

04 · Development Process

From research plan to installable PWA

01

Research

Defined persona, core hypotheses, and success metrics before any UI work began.

02

Plan

Scoped a strict MVP feature set and explicitly listed what the product would not include.

03

Design

Built the interface around a single daily view, with insights and settings kept one tap away.

04

Build

Implemented with vanilla HTML/CSS/JS, IndexedDB persistence, a manifest, and a service worker.

05

Verify

Tested installability, offline behaviour, and manifest/service-worker health in DevTools before shipping.

06 · Design Principles

Four rules that shaped every decision

01

Zero friction

No account, no paywall, no setup — a habit can be logged within seconds of opening the app.

02

Offline by default

Core functionality works with no connection, backed by a service worker and IndexedDB.

03

Privacy as a feature

No analytics, no tracking, no cloud storage — data stays on the user's device.

04

Small and maintainable

Vanilla JS and standard CSS over frameworks, kept deliberately simple to extend.

07 · Final Product

The finished PWA

The shipped product includes a Today dashboard with streaks and a progress ring, a 30-day insights heatmap, light/dark themes, JSON export/import, and full install-to-home-screen support.

10 · Reflection

What I learned

Constraint-driven design is still design. Deciding what HabitFlow would not include — accounts, cloud sync, social feeds — did as much for the product as any single screen. The hardest part wasn't the interface; it was staying disciplined about scope.

Working with an AI-driven development workflow also meant documenting decisions as I made them, not after the fact — which made the research plan, design principles, and final build far easier to keep honest with each other.

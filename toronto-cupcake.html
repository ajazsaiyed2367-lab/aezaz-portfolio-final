01 · Overview

Designing a sweeter first impression.

Toronto Cupcake's website is built around fresh cupcakes, seasonal products, celebrations, custom designs, and delivery. The existing homepage communicates a lot of information, but a landing page can work harder by establishing one clear visual story and a stronger path toward ordering.

This redesign keeps the playful personality of the brand while restructuring the experience around discovery, trust, and action.

"The goal was not to remove the personality of the brand — it was to make that personality easier to understand and act on."

02 · Research & Discovery

Understanding the existing experience

I reviewed the public-facing Toronto Cupcake homepage to understand its content, navigation, product offering, messaging, and ordering journey. The live site highlights fresh baking, occasions, custom designs, delivery, seasonal products, and online ordering.

01

Strong personality

Colourful imagery and cupcake-focused content give the brand an approachable, celebratory character.

02

Many offerings

Visitors may be shopping for everyday flavours, holidays, corporate events, birthdays, weddings, or custom orders.

03

Multiple intents

The homepage needs to support browsing, occasion discovery, customisation, and immediate ordering.

04

Delivery matters

Freshness and reliable delivery are important trust signals for an online cupcake purchase.

Source reviewed: TorontoCupcake.com

03 · Problem

How might we make the homepage simpler without losing the brand's charm?

The core challenge was balancing a visually rich bakery brand with the practical needs of an ecommerce visitor: understand the offer quickly, find a relevant product, build confidence, and know what to do next.

01

Competing attention

Several messages can compete for the visitor's attention instead of creating one clear first step.

02

Content density

A broad product range needs stronger grouping and scanning patterns.

03

Conversion path

The route from discovering the brand to choosing and ordering a cupcake can be more direct.

06 · Design Principles

Four rules shaped the redesign

01

Clarity first

Every section should have a purpose and a clear visual priority.

02

Show, don't overload

Use product photography and concise copy to communicate quickly.

03

One primary action

Ordering remains the strongest action while secondary paths stay quieter.

04

Keep the personality

Pastel colour, playful type, and cupcake imagery preserve the emotional brand character.

10 · Reflection

What I learned

A redesign is not only about making an interface look better. The visual system needs to support the user's decision-making process. For an ecommerce homepage, imagery, headlines, cards, and CTAs should all help visitors understand what is available and what to do next.

The next step would be usability testing with real users to validate ordering confidence, product discovery, and task completion, followed by iteration based on observed behaviour.

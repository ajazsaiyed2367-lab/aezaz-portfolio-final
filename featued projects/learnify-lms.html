01 · Overview

Making online learning easier to understand.

The supplied Learnify LMS screen presents a complete marketing story: a hero introduction, trusted brands, achievement metrics, powerful features, testimonials, a productivity CTA, and a dark footer.

This case study uses that exact supplied design as the single project reference and translates the visible content structure into a UX case-study narrative rather than splitting it into separate projects.

"The best LMS experience makes learning tools feel like support, not administration."

03 · Problem

How might we communicate a feature-rich LMS without making the experience feel complicated?

An LMS can contain many capabilities while serving multiple audiences. The landing page therefore has to establish credibility quickly, explain benefits without overwhelming visitors, and maintain a clear path toward getting started.

01

Feature density

Course management, assessment, grading, and collaboration can create cognitive load when presented without hierarchy.

02

Multiple audiences

Institutions, teachers, and students each need to understand a different part of the platform's value.

03

Conversion clarity

The page needs to move naturally from credibility and features toward one confident action.

06 · Design Principles

Four rules shaped the experience

01

Build trust early

Credibility signals should appear before visitors reach detailed feature content.

02

Explain through visuals

Large learning imagery and concise copy make feature benefits easier to scan.

03

Keep actions obvious

The "Get started" action remains visually distinct and easy to recognize.

04

Keep it human

People-focused imagery, testimonials, and generous whitespace make the LMS feel approachable.

10 · Reflection

What I learned

A strong LMS landing page needs to sell confidence before it sells features. The supplied Learnify design already contains useful proof points; the UX opportunity is to connect those proof points into a narrative that helps visitors understand why the platform matters.

The next step would be usability testing with educators, students, and institutional decision-makers to validate feature comprehension, trust, accessibility, and CTA effectiveness.
